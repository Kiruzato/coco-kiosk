THE PROBLEM
===========

1. Section merging combined multiple admin sections into one "Deans" chunk (chunk 69)
   - Merged 17 administrative sections total (671 → 654 sections)
   - Created 738 chunks (down from 752 in original Phase 18)

2. The merged "Deans" chunk content starts with:
   "Director, Alumni & External Affairs Director, Campus Ministry
    Director, Community Extension Services
    Director, Quality Assurance & Change Management
    VP for Academic Affairs and Student Services
    ..."
   
   The actual dean names (Matriano, Yap, Gonzales, Gutierrez, Capili) appear 
   later in the chunk content.

3. Current retrieval configuration:
   - K=8 (retrieves top 8 chunks)
   - Score threshold: 0.5
   - Hybrid scoring: 70% vector + 30% keyword

4. All relevant chunks ARE within top 8:
   - Rank #2: Almazan (dedicated chunk)
   - Rank #3: Main "Deans" chunk (contains 5 deans)
   - Rank #8: Capili (separate chunk)

5. LLM receives these chunks but only extracts 4/6 deans in the answer

WHY ONLY 4/6 DEANS APPEAR
==========================

Analysis of LLM behavior:
- The 4 deans (Matriano, Yap, Gonzales, Gutierrez) are clearly listed in the "Deans" chunk
- Almazan has her own dedicated chunk (rank #2) ✓ but still missing from answer
- Capili appears in BOTH:
  * The merged "Deans" chunk (rank #3) 
  * A separate chunk (rank #8)
  But is still missing from answer

Hypothesis:
The LLM may not be extracting all dean names from the merged chunk because:
1. The chunk content is diluted with director/VP information
2. The dean names appear later in the chunk (not at the beginning)
3. The chunk is large and the LLM may be focusing on early content

SECTION MERGING BEHAVIOR
=========================

The merge_related_admin_sections() function:
- Identifies sections containing admin keywords (dean, director, president, etc.)
- Merges adjacent admin sections within 2 pages
- Prioritizes "Deans" keyword in merged section titles
- Successfully renamed merged section to "Deans" (clean title)

However, the content ordering within merged chunks may be suboptimal for enumeration queries.

POTENTIAL SOLUTIONS
===================

Option 1: Increase K from 8 to 10
- Pros: Ensures all dean chunks are well within retrieval window
- Cons: More noise, may affect other queries
- Alignment: Standard RAG practice (K=10-20 common)

Option 2: Improve chunk content ordering
- Pros: Dean names appear first in merged chunks
- Cons: Requires modifying create_chunks_from_sections logic
- Alignment: Content-aware chunking best practice

Option 3: Split overly-merged chunks
- Pros: Each dean gets clearer context
- Cons: May fragment related information
- Alignment: Depends on chunk size constraints

Option 4: Adjust section merging criteria
- Pros: More selective merging, preserve dedicated dean sections
- Cons: May lose benefits of consolidation
- Alignment: Balance between structure and granularity

Option 5: Improve hybrid scoring weights
- Pros: Boost keyword matching for enumeration queries
- Cons: May affect other query types
- Alignment: Query-type-specific tuning

RECOMMENDATION
==============

Recommended approach: Option 1 (Increase K to 10) + Option 4 (Refine merging)

Rationale:
1. K=8 is too tight for enumeration queries with 6+ entities
2. Industry standard for RAG is K=10-20 for comprehensive retrieval
3. Refine merging to avoid combining "Deans" section with unrelated directors
4. This is a minimal, architecture-aligned change

Implementation:
1. Change RETRIEVAL_TOP_K from 8 to 10 in app.py
2. Modify merge_related_admin_sections to NOT merge sections with "Deans" as exact title
3. Re-ingest and verify all 6 deans appear
