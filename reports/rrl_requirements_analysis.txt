================================================================================
PRIORITY: CRITICAL (Must be supported by RRL)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RETRIEVAL-AUGMENTED GENERATION (RAG) ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  RAG is the foundational paradigm of the system. The decision to combine
  retrieval with generation, rather than rely on pure generative models,
  requires theoretical grounding to justify why this approach reduces
  hallucination and improves factual accuracy for domain-specific applications.
  Claims about RAG's superiority over zero-shot generation for institutional
  knowledge must be supported.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Original RAG papers (Lewis et al., 2020)
  - Studies on LLM hallucination and factual grounding
  - Comparative studies of pure-generative vs. retrieval-augmented approaches
  - Domain-specific RAG implementations (education, enterprise)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. HYBRID RETRIEVAL: VECTOR + KEYWORD FUSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system implements 70/30 weighted hybrid scoring and Reciprocal Rank
  Fusion (RRF) to combine dense vector similarity with sparse keyword matching.
  This design decision assumes that pure semantic search is insufficient for
  domain-specific terminology (acronyms, proper nouns). This claim requires
  empirical and theoretical backing from information retrieval literature.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Dense-sparse hybrid retrieval studies
  - Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) foundational work
  - BM25 and term-frequency scoring research
  - Studies on vocabulary mismatch in semantic search
  - Comparative evaluations of retrieval strategies

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. GROUNDING AND ANSWER VALIDATION MECHANISMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system enforces "grounding validation" requiring query terms to appear
  in retrieved chunks before allowing LLM generation. This is a core safety
  mechanism that defines when the system will answer vs. refuse. The semantic
  override logic (similarity >= 0.75, content >= 300 chars) for long-form
  content is a novel threshold-based decision that needs methodological
  justification.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Grounding in language models
  - Attribution and citation in AI systems
  - Factual consistency verification methods
  - Confidence calibration in retrieval systems
  - Threshold-based decision making in IR

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. DETERMINISTIC EXTRACTION VS. LLM-BASED REASONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system makes an explicit architectural choice to bypass LLM generation
  for high-stakes factual queries (directory locations, event dates, personnel
  listings) in favor of regex-based extraction. This hybrid human-AI approach
  requires justification through literature on:
  (a) limitations of generative models for structured data
  (b) advantages of rule-based systems for specific retrieval tasks
  (c) hybrid system architectures in applied AI

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Neuro-symbolic AI and hybrid architectures
  - Rule-based vs. statistical NLP approaches
  - Information extraction methodologies
  - Precision vs. recall trade-offs in applied systems
  - Pattern-based entity extraction

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CONFIDENCE SCORING AND REFUSE-TO-ANSWER BEHAVIOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system assigns confidence levels (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW) and refuses to answer
  when confidence falls below threshold. This "I don't know" behavior is a
  critical design choice for institutional trust. The methodology for computing
  confidence (average similarity, max similarity, grounding mode) needs to be
  grounded in literature on uncertainty quantification and calibrated AI.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Uncertainty estimation in neural networks
  - Calibrated confidence in AI systems
  - Abstention and selective prediction
  - Trust in AI systems
  - Risk-aware question answering

PRIORITY: IMPORTANT (Strongly recommended for RRL)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. VOICE INTERFACE INTEGRATION (STT/TTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system integrates Speech-to-Text (Whisper) and Text-to-Speech (Piper)
  for kiosk accessibility. Voice is treated as a "modality layer" that does
  not alter RAG guarantees. This design decision and the choice of offline-
  first voice engines require justification through accessibility and HCI
  literature.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Voice user interfaces (VUI) design
  - Accessibility in public kiosk systems
  - Offline speech recognition systems
  - Neural network-based TTS (Piper, etc.)
  - Multi-modal interaction design

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. PDF/DOCUMENT INGESTION AND LAYOUT-AWARE PARSING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system implements multi-stage text normalization (NFKC, hyphen cleanup,
  artifact removal) and consolidation of fragmented content (prayers, lists).
  Claims that PDF parsing artifacts degrade retrieval quality should be
  supported by document understanding literature.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Document layout understanding
  - PDF text extraction challenges
  - Text normalization and preprocessing
  - Document chunking strategies
  - Layout-aware information retrieval

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. KIOSK DEPLOYMENT AND RASPBERRY PI CONSTRAINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system is designed for edge deployment on Raspberry Pi 5 with offline-
  first operation. This addresses latency, cost, and reliability concerns
  specific to public-facing kiosk environments. The trade-offs (local vs.
  cloud inference, model size constraints) need embedded systems and edge
  AI justification.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Edge AI and on-device inference
  - Embedded systems for machine learning
  - Resource-constrained NLP
  - Kiosk and public terminal design
  - Offline-first system architectures

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. ENTITY CONSOLIDATION AND SYNTHETIC CHUNK CREATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system pre-aggregates related content (e.g., all deans, complete prayers)
  into "synthetic chunks" at ingestion time to ensure complete retrieval. This
  addresses the well-known "chunking problem" in RAG systems where entities
  span multiple chunks. The consolidation framework (config-driven pattern
  matching) is a methodological contribution.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Chunking strategies for RAG systems
  - Entity-centric document representation
  - Knowledge compilation and pre-processing
  - Multi-hop retrieval challenges
  - Document structure exploitation

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. CONVERSATION MEMORY AND MULTI-TURN INTERACTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Required:
  The system implements session-scoped conversation memory with 5-turn windows
  and 30-minute timeouts. Follow-up resolution ("What floor is it on?") and
  disambiguation (numbered choice selection) are explicit design features that
  require dialogue systems literature support.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Conversational AI and dialogue management
  - Context tracking in multi-turn systems
  - Coreference resolution in dialogue
  - Session management in chatbots
  - Disambiguation strategies

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. QUERY SYNONYM EXPANSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Beneficial:
  The system implements domain-specific synonym expansion (~40 bidirectional
  mappings) to improve recall for informal queries (e.g., "CR" -> "restroom").
  While effective, this is a well-established technique that can be briefly
  justified without extensive literature review.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Query expansion techniques
  - Synonym-based information retrieval
  - Domain adaptation in NLP

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. CHUNK DIVERSITY AND SECTION DEDUPLICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Beneficial:
  The system limits chunks per section (max 3) to prevent same-section
  dominance. Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) is implemented for future use.
  These are standard IR techniques with established literature.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Result diversification in IR
  - Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR)
  - Redundancy removal in retrieval

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. NEGATIVE EXAMPLES AND ENTITY CONFUSION DETECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Beneficial:
  The system detects semantic neighbor confusion (e.g., "Dean's List" vs.
  "Team Leadership Award") using predefined confusion pairs. This is a
  practical guardrail that can reference literature on semantic similarity
  failures and false positive handling.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Semantic similarity pitfalls
  - Negative sampling in retrieval
  - False positive detection in IR

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. ADMIN OBSERVABILITY AND ANALYTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Beneficial:
  The system logs query events, refusals, and confidence scores for admin
  review. This is standard software engineering practice but can reference
  literature on AI system monitoring and feedback loops for content
  improvement.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - AI system observability
  - Human-in-the-loop AI maintenance
  - Feedback loops in production ML

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. UX DESIGN FOR TRUST AND CLARITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why RRL is Beneficial:
  The kiosk UI shows confidence badges, source citations, and grounding mode
  to build user trust. These design choices can reference HCI literature on
  transparency and explainability in AI interfaces.

Relevant Literature Types:
  - Explainable AI interfaces
  - Trust in AI systems
  - Transparency in automated systems
  - Kiosk UX design patterns
