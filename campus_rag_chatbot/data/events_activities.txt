Campus Events and Activities - Springfield University

Student Organizations

Registered Student Organizations
Springfield University has over 200 registered student organizations covering academic, cultural, recreational, and special interest areas. Organizations include academic honor societies, cultural clubs, service organizations, recreational sports clubs, and hobby groups. Students can join existing organizations or start new ones by submitting a registration application to the Office of Student Life. All organizations must have a faculty advisor and maintain active status by participating in at least 4 campus events per year.

Greek Life
The university has 12 fraternities and 10 sororities, including both social and professional Greek organizations. Approximately 15% of undergraduate students participate in Greek life. Recruitment (Rush) occurs at the beginning of each semester. Greek organizations are governed by the Interfraternity Council and Panhellenic Council. All Greek organizations must maintain a minimum chapter GPA of 2.7. Many chapters have housing facilities near campus.

Student Government
The Student Government Association (SGA) represents student interests and organizes campus-wide programs and events. SGA has an elected president, vice president, and senators representing each college and class year. Elections are held each April for the following academic year. SGA manages a student activity fee budget of approximately $500,000 that funds student organizations, campus events, and student services. Students can attend SGA meetings, which are held every Tuesday at 6:00 PM in the Student Union.

Campus Events

Weekly and Monthly Events
The Campus Activities Board organizes weekly events including movie nights every Friday in the Student Union Theater, trivia nights on Wednesdays at the campus pub, and live music performances on Thursdays at the outdoor amphitheater (weather permitting). Monthly events include comedy shows, cultural celebrations, and guest speaker presentations. Most events are free for students with a valid student ID.

Annual Traditions
Springfield University hosts several annual traditions. Homecoming Weekend occurs in October and features a parade, football game, alumni gatherings, and a formal dance. SpringFest is a week-long celebration in April with concerts, carnival games, food vendors, and outdoor activities. The university also holds an annual Talent Show in February and a Campus Lip Sync Battle in November. Senior Week includes special events exclusively for graduating seniors including a senior formal, senior breakfast, and senior awards ceremony.

Guest Speakers and Lectures
The Distinguished Speaker Series brings notable authors, politicians, scientists, and activists to campus 4-5 times per year. Recent speakers have included Pulitzer Prize-winning journalists, CEOs of major corporations, and social justice advocates. All lectures are free and open to the public. The university also hosts specialized lecture series in individual colleges, such as the Engineering Innovation Talks and the Business Leadership Forum.

Arts and Culture

Performing Arts
The Department of Theatre and Dance produces four main stage productions each academic year, typically including a musical, contemporary drama, classical play, and student-directed works. Tickets are $10 for students and $15 for general admission. The Music Department hosts over 50 concerts annually featuring student ensembles, faculty recitals, and guest artists. Performances take place in the 800-seat Williams Concert Hall. Student organizations also produce independent theatrical and musical performances throughout the year.

Art Galleries
The University Art Gallery in the Fine Arts Building features rotating exhibitions of contemporary art, student work, and historical collections. The gallery is free and open to the public Tuesday through Sunday from 11:00 AM to 5:00 PM. The gallery hosts opening receptions for new exhibitions on the first Friday of each month from 5:00 PM to 7:00 PM with refreshments and artist talks.

Film Screenings
The Film Society screens independent, foreign, and classic films every Tuesday and Thursday at 7:00 PM in the Media Studies Center. Screenings are free for students and $5 for the general public. The society also hosts an annual Student Film Festival in April showcasing short films created by Springfield University students.

Recreational Activities

Intramural Sports
The Campus Recreation Department offers intramural sports leagues for students, faculty, and staff. Sports include flag football, soccer, basketball, volleyball, ultimate frisbee, and softball. Leagues are organized by skill level (recreational and competitive) and run throughout the academic year. Teams can be formed through student organizations, residence halls, or groups of friends. Registration fees are $20 per team. Championship t-shirts are awarded to winning teams.

Club Sports
Springfield University has 25 club sports teams including lacrosse, rugby, ice hockey, equestrian, martial arts, and sailing. Club sports compete against other universities in regional competitions. Unlike varsity athletics, club sports are student-run with assistance from the Campus Recreation Department. Students of all skill levels are welcome to join. Most clubs practice 2-3 times per week and compete in 4-6 tournaments per semester.

Outdoor Recreation
The Outdoor Adventure Program organizes hiking, camping, rock climbing, kayaking, and skiing trips throughout the year. Weekend trips cost $30-$80 and include transportation, equipment, and instruction. Popular trips include fall foliage hikes, winter skiing in nearby mountains, and spring break camping. The program also offers outdoor skills workshops such as wilderness first aid, map and compass navigation, and Leave No Trace principles. Equipment rentals are available for students planning their own outdoor adventures.

Wellness Programs

Fitness Classes
The Wellness Center offers over 30 group fitness classes per week including yoga, Pilates, Zumba, kickboxing, cycling, and strength training. Classes are free for all students and are taught by certified instructors. Class schedules are available online and through the campus recreation mobile app. Popular classes fill up quickly, so students are encouraged to arrive 10-15 minutes early.

Mindfulness and Meditation
The Wellness Center hosts free mindfulness and meditation sessions every Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at noon in the Wellness Studio. Sessions last 30 minutes and are suitable for beginners and experienced practitioners. The center also offers workshops on stress management, sleep hygiene, and healthy relationships throughout the semester.

Wellness Challenges
The Wellness Center runs semesterly wellness challenges encouraging students to develop healthy habits. Past challenges have focused on step counts, hydration, sleep, and gratitude journaling. Participants who complete challenges are entered into raffles for prizes including fitness equipment, wellness center swag, and local business gift cards.

Volunteer and Service Opportunities

Community Service
The Center for Community Engagement connects students with volunteer opportunities in the local community. Partnerships include tutoring programs at local schools, food banks, animal shelters, environmental conservation projects, and senior centers. The center maintains a database of ongoing volunteer opportunities and one-time service projects. Students can log volunteer hours to earn the President's Volunteer Service Award.

Alternative Break Programs
Alternative break programs offer students opportunities to engage in service during fall break, winter break, and spring break. Recent trips have included disaster relief work, building homes with Habitat for Humanity, environmental conservation in national parks, and educational programs in underserved communities. Trips typically cost $200-$400 and include housing, meals, and transportation.

Service Learning Courses
Many academic courses incorporate service learning components where students apply classroom knowledge to real-world community challenges. Service learning courses are designated in the course catalog and typically require 20-40 hours of community engagement throughout the semester. Projects have included marketing plans for nonprofits, environmental assessments for conservation groups, and health education programs for community centers.
