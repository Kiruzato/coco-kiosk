Student Employment Opportunities - Springfield University

On-Campus Employment

Work-Study Program
The Federal Work-Study program provides part-time jobs for undergraduate and graduate students with financial need. Students can work up to 20 hours per week during the academic year and up to 40 hours during breaks. Positions are available in dining services, libraries, administrative offices, and academic departments. Work-study awards are included in financial aid packages. Students must apply for positions through the Career Services portal.

Student Assistant Positions
Departments across campus hire student assistants for various roles including administrative support, event planning, tutoring, and research assistance. Most positions pay $12-15 per hour. Applications are accepted throughout the year, with most hiring occurring before each semester. Check the student employment website at jobs.springfield.edu for current openings.

Research Assistantships
Undergraduate and graduate students can work as research assistants in faculty labs and research centers. Positions typically require 10-15 hours per week and pay $13-18 per hour depending on experience. Research assistantships provide valuable hands-on experience and can lead to co-authorship on publications. Contact individual faculty members or department offices to inquire about opportunities.

Teaching Assistantships
Graduate students may serve as teaching assistants (TAs) for undergraduate courses. TA responsibilities include leading discussion sections, grading assignments, holding office hours, and assisting with course preparation. Full TAs receive tuition remission and a stipend of $18,000 per academic year. Part-time TA positions are also available.

Campus Recreation Jobs
The Athletic Center hires students for positions including lifeguards, fitness instructors, intramural officials, and equipment room attendants. Lifeguards must have current lifeguard certification. Fitness instructors should have group fitness certification. Pay ranges from $11-16 per hour. Applications open in April for the following academic year.

Dining Services
University Dining hires over 200 student employees each year for positions in dining halls, cafes, and catering. Positions include cashiers, food service workers, and baristas. Dining services offers flexible scheduling around class times. Starting pay is $12 per hour with opportunities for advancement. Benefits include free meals during shifts.

Library Student Workers
The university library employs students for circulation desk, shelving, archives assistance, and digital resources support. Library positions offer quiet work environments conducive to studying during downtime. Students work 8-15 hours per week at $12 per hour. Positions are posted in August and December.

Off-Campus Employment

Part-Time Jobs
The Career Services Office maintains a database of off-campus part-time employment opportunities from local businesses. Common positions include retail, food service, tutoring, and administrative work. Students should ensure off-campus work does not interfere with academic responsibilities. International students must consult International Student Services before accepting off-campus employment.

Internships and Co-ops
Career Services helps students find semester-long internships and cooperative education (co-op) experiences. Internships provide professional work experience in a student's field of study. Many internships are paid, with hourly rates ranging from $15-30 depending on the industry. Co-ops are typically full-time positions that may extend for multiple semesters. Engineering students participate in mandatory co-op experiences.

Summer Employment
Students can work full-time during summer break either on-campus or through external internships. Many academic departments keep their student employees over the summer. Summer research programs provide stipends of $4,000-6,000 for 10 weeks of research work. Career Services hosts a Summer Job Fair in February.

Application Process

How to Apply
Most on-campus positions are posted on the student employment portal at jobs.springfield.edu. Students create a profile, upload their resume, and apply directly through the system. Some positions may require a cover letter or additional application materials. Applications are typically due 2-3 weeks after posting.

Interview Preparation
Career Services offers interview preparation workshops and mock interviews. Students can schedule one-on-one sessions with career counselors to practice interview skills. Common interview questions for student positions focus on time management, reliability, and relevant skills. Professional dress is typically not required for student employment interviews.

Work Authorization
All students must complete I-9 employment verification before starting work. U.S. citizens and permanent residents should bring a driver's license and Social Security card. International students must visit International Student Services to ensure they have proper work authorization. F-1 visa students can work on-campus up to 20 hours per week.

Payroll and Taxes
Student employees are paid bi-weekly via direct deposit. Students must set up direct deposit through the HR portal. All employees receive a W-2 form in January for tax filing. Students working on-campus are exempt from FICA taxes if enrolled at least half-time. The Payroll Office can answer questions about paychecks and tax withholding.

Balancing Work and Academics
The university recommends students work no more than 15-20 hours per week to maintain academic success. Students should prioritize coursework and communicate with supervisors about exam schedules and heavy assignment periods. Supervisors understand that students are employees second and students first. Poor academic performance may result in loss of work-study eligibility.

Employee Resources
Student employees have access to employee benefits including free campus recreation center access (if not already included in student fees), employee discounts at the campus bookstore, and professional development workshops. The Office of Human Resources provides new employee orientation covering workplace expectations, safety procedures, and university policies.

Career Development Benefits
Student employment provides more than just income. On-campus jobs help students develop time management, communication, teamwork, and problem-solving skills valued by future employers. Supervisors can serve as references for future job applications. Many students find that on-campus employment leads to post-graduation career opportunities through networking and skill development.
